The Only Constant

Every Formula 1 season since the championship began in 1950 has had one thing in common: a scarlet car on the grid. Manufacturers have come and gone — Mercedes, McLaren, Williams, Red Bull have all risen and fallen — but Ferrari has never missed a season. It is the sport's only ever-present team, and by almost every measure, its most successful.

Ferrari holds the record for the most Constructors' Championships, the most Drivers' Championships, the most race wins, and the most pole positions in Formula 1 history. The Prancing Horse is not just a team; it is the institution against which every other is measured.

And yet, for the better part of twenty years, the most decorated name in motorsport has been on the outside looking in — close, sometimes agonisingly close, but never quite over the line. This is the story of how Ferrari reached the summit, and how long it has been trying to find its way back.

1950

It starts at the start

When the Formula 1 World Championship was founded, Ferrari was there. Seventy-five years later, it is the only team that can still say so — present for every era the sport has ever had.

2000 — 2004

The golden age

With Michael Schumacher, Ferrari built the most dominant dynasty F1 had ever seen: five consecutive Drivers' Championships and six straight Constructors' titles. The red cars didn't just win — they redefined what winning looked like.

2007 — 2008

The last summits

Kimi Räikkönen delivered Ferrari's most recent Drivers' Championship in 2007. The team's most recent Constructors' Championship followed in 2008. At the time, no one imagined how long the wait would be.

2008 → Today

The gap opens

From that last title, the seasons began to stack up. Rivals reinvented themselves and rose to dominance. Ferrari kept building cars, kept fighting — but the top step of the championship stayed just out of reach.

Total dominance

In both 2002 and 2004, Ferrari won 15 of the season's races. In 2004, Schumacher alone took 13 victories, including 12 of the first 13 rounds. The Constructors' title was often decided with months to spare.

The Drought

Close, but never first

The wait has not been for lack of trying. In 2010 and again in 2012, Fernando Alonso carried Ferrari to the final round of the season still in title contention — only to fall short. In 2017 and 2018, Sebastian Vettel led championships that slipped away mid-season. Each near miss added another year to the count.

Only Ever-Present Team

The wait goes on

With seven-time champion Lewis Hamilton alongside Charles Leclerc, Ferrari has assembled one of the most talked-about line-ups in its history. The question that has hung over Maranello for nearly two decades remains the same — and the whole of Formula 1 is watching to see if this is the year the long wait finally ends.
